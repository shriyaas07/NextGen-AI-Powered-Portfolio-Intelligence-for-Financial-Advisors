About this tool

Once you have built and saved multiple portfolios in the Optimizer, this page lets you place any two side by side for a direct comparison returns, risk, efficiency, and top holdings so you can decide which fits your goals before committing real money.

What the metrics mean

Return (%)

Expected annual growth. Higher is not automatically better — a 20% return portfolio can still be worse than a 15% one if it carries far more risk.

Volatility (%)

How unstable the portfolio's value is. If seeing a 20% monthly drop would make you panic-sell, choose the lower-volatility option.

Sharpe Ratio

The most important metric for comparison. Return earned per unit of risk. Always prefer the higher Sharpe — it is the more efficient portfolio.

Holdings Count

More holdings = more diversified = less damage if any single stock tanks.

Top Holdings

The 5 largest positions in each portfolio. Shared top stocks means the two portfolios are more similar than they appear. Very different holdings means they complement each other well if you split your money between both.

How to use

  1. 1 Save at least 2 portfolios first — go to the Optimizer, run it with different settings, and save at least 2 with different names.
  2. 2 Select Portfolio A and B — use the dropdowns to choose the two you want to compare.
  3. 3 Click "Compare" — two detailed cards appear side by side with all metrics and top holdings.
  4. 4 Pick your winner — for safety, choose lower volatility. For growth, choose higher Sharpe and return. When unsure, the Balanced portfolio is a reliable default.

Pro tip

Compare a High Return portfolio against a Low Risk one. You will see exactly how chasing higher returns always trades off against volatility — the fundamental rule of all investing.

Comparison is based on historical optimization data. Past performance does not guarantee future results.
